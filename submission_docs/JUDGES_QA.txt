TIME HAS A PRICE — QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

What did you build?
A mathematical decision model that uses observed market data to compare continuing, buying more time budget, and stopping. The interface explains the choices and exposes the evidence.

How does this fit the Time theme?
Time changes the feasible actions and the objective. Every market day consumes budget, purchases extend the decision horizon, and unused budget has value on exit.

How do you combine money and time?
U = alpha × ln(1 + L/90) + (1 − alpha) × ln(1 + B/100).
L is remaining budget, B is cash after costs and purchases, and alpha is time preference. Logarithms give diminishing marginal value. Budget exhaustion has utility zero. These are transparent assumptions, not a universal score for human life.

How does the model choose to buy or exit?
It computes each action’s expected utility before choosing. Buying must beat exit and every feasible action without a purchase. If no continuation action beats exit, it stops. Future actions can respond to information only after that information is observed.

Does 30-step planning predict the next 30 prices?
No. Thirty steps is the decision horizon. Future outcomes come from a frozen empirical distribution, not the realized replay path. Signals use past data and orders are chosen before the next opening price is read.

Why can you solve the longer problem? Is it exact?
We merge reachable states. Remaining budget follows from elapsed days and packs bought; portfolio fractions have a finite set of reachable nodes. Only wealth needs grid interpolation. This solves the full finite-horizon model approximately in wealth, not the real market to global optimality.

What does the result show?
At the primary assumptions, paying to wait is not worthwhile and the model exits. Longer planning changes some fixed-state decisions, but the historical evidence does not establish stable improvement. Refusing an opportunity is a valid answer to a stopping problem; this is not a demonstrated profitable trading strategy.

Are the 61,344 episodes independent tests?
No. They combine parameter settings, controllers and highly overlapping windows. Each asset has 213 overlapping starts per condition. We report paired block-bootstrap intervals and a non-overlapping-start sensitivity check. Mean episode cash is not a continuous account’s annual return.

Was 2026 a blind test?
The market kernel was estimated on 2022–2025 data and frozen, but the 2026 data had already been inspected. This is historical re-evaluation. Genuinely unseen data are needed for the next test.

Is the EMA comparison fair?
The constrained EMA and mechanical time-buying EMA share the same costs, safety rules and terminal conversion. The original EMA without a time budget is listed separately as a cash reference; its utility is not comparable. The public 15/30 EMA signal is credited.

Why separate purchases during trading from purchases after exit?
A purchase during trading can make future decisions possible. A purchase after exit changes final budget but creates no further trading opportunity. Paid prices 8 and 2 produce no mid-episode purchases; free purchases do not show that paid time is worthwhile.

Is this about buying real lifespan?
No lifespan data or medical effects are used. Budget seconds are model units. Prices are observed; cash-to-budget exchange rates are assumptions. The framework can illustrate finite budgets in research, computation, experiments or waiting for opportunities.

How did you check the implementation?
Synthetic tests cover planning and boundary cases. A separate implementation audits balances, fees, budget conservation, terminal purchases and summary statistics. At the primary price, action choices are stable between wealth-grid spacing 1 and 0.5. These checks do not prove the market distribution remains valid.

What comes from open source or AI?
Market data come from Binance; the EMA signal references QuantConnect LEAN with attribution. Our contribution combines time budgets, purchasable duration and stopping in an inspectable comparison, with a 30-step solver and analysis interface. AI assisted coding, analysis and interface production.

What would you do next?
Freeze the rules and evaluate unseen data, then test whether market-state definitions and utility assumptions change the conclusion.
