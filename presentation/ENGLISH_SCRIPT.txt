TIME HAS A PRICE — BROWSER DEMO
Suggested 2–3-minute walkthrough. Keep the report open throughout.
Presenter A introduces and concludes. Presenter B explains the model and operates the browser.

BEFORE THE JUDGE ARRIVES
Open the report and click “Reset baseline”. Close optional panels.
Check BTC, time-pack price 8, alpha 0.1, and initial budget 90.
Use browser full screen and a readable zoom. Save code for questions.

0:00–0:25 | THE QUESTION | PRESENTER A
“Most trading models ask how much money we can make. We ask what waiting costs.
Time Has a Price is a mathematical decision lab built on real market data.
With limited time, when should we keep trading, buy more time, or stop?”

0:25–0:55 | THE MODEL | PRESENTER B
“Each account starts with one hundred cash and a time budget.
Every market day uses five budget seconds. One time pack adds fifteen seconds.
The model can exit, hold cash, or invest half or all its wealth. It can also buy a pack before continuing.
Our score values both final cash and remaining time.”

0:55–1:25 | THE BASELINE | PRESENTER A
Point to the strategy comparison.
“We estimated a market model from Bitcoin prices for 2022 to 2025 and froze it.
We then replayed Bitcoin and Ethereum history from 2026.
At this baseline, both planners exit with one hundred cash and ninety budget seconds.
The budget-limited moving-average strategy keeps about one hundred point zero nine cash, but only five seconds.
More cash alone does not settle the decision.”

1:25–2:10 | A DECISION THAT CHANGES | PRESENTER B
Scroll to “When does more time change the decision?” Click “Show a changed decision”.
“Here the starting budget is forty-five seconds.
The two-step planner exits. The thirty-step planner continues fully invested.
It values future chances to observe and choose again; it does not know future prices.
Longer planning changes six of one hundred and ninety-two fixed-state decisions.
But changing a decision does not guarantee a better outcome. This historical utility difference is slightly negative, and its uncertainty interval includes zero. Improvement is unconfirmed.”
Point to the action cards and the interval statement. Leave optional panels closed.

2:10–2:40 | WHAT WE LEARNED | PRESENTER A
“The thirty-step planner made no paid time purchases during trading in these replays.
Some purchases after exiting are final time consumption, not extra trading time.
Budget seconds and time prices are our assumptions. The 2026 history had already been inspected, so we still need an unseen test.
We built a thirty-step optimizer to discover when the best trade is to leave.”

IF ASKED ABOUT THE MATH
Open “Methods and checks”.
“Our score combines logarithmic cash utility and time utility. Extra units have diminishing value.
Bellman planning compares each action’s expected future score, allowing another decision after new information arrives.
We use an empirical market model and an interpolated wealth grid, not an exact solution for the real market.”

IF ASKED WHEN BUYING TIME IS WORTHWHILE
Open “Price and preferences”. Set price 2, alpha 0.1, and initial budget 20.
“The model exits and exchanges six cash for three time packs: ninety-four cash and sixty-five budget seconds.
This is a terminal exchange. It does not extend trading.”

IF THE LIVE REPORT FAILS
Open the packaged demo using RUN_DEMO.command or “python3 serve.py --open”.
If that is unavailable, explain the saved baseline and changed decision verbally. Do not debug during the pitch.

USE THESE TERMS
Budget seconds; expected utility; historical replay; improvement unconfirmed.
Avoid: proven profit, real lifespan, blind test, independent trials, or thirty-day price prediction.
