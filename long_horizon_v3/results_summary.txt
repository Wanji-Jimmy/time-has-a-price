Findings
1. At price 8 and alpha 0.1, the 30-step planner exits immediately in all eight asset/initial-budget conditions. Preserving budget improves utility; this does not mean earning more cash.
2. Longer planning changes 6 of 192 fixed initial-state actions. At price 8, alpha 0.02, initial budget 45 and cash 100 in a falling, high-volatility state, two steps choose exit while 30 steps choose full investment.
3. Under those parameters, the BTC realized utility difference, 30-step minus 2-step, is −0.000708. The 95% block-30 interval is [−0.003254, +0.001074], so improvement is not established.
4. At paid prices 8 and 2, no replay account buys time during trading. At price 0, 51 accounts make 65 purchases.
5. At price 2, alpha 0.1 and initial budget 20, the planner immediately exits and buys three packs at settlement: final cash 94 and budget 65. This is terminal consumption, not an extension of trading.
6. At primary price 8, all action choices agree between wealth-grid spacing 1 and 0.5 across 64 fixed states and 1,139 unique historical decision states. This is an observed stability check, not a rigorous error bound.
7. The independent audit passes for 61,344 accounts, 391,772 ledger events, 288 means, 240 paired differences and 480 block-bootstrap intervals.

Read point estimates with their uncertainty intervals. The 213 starts overlap and are not independent. Prices are observed; budget seconds, prices for time, exhaustion penalties and utility preferences are assumptions. Q values are model estimates.
